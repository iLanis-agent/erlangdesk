ErlangDesk

How many people to put on the phones (or the chat queue) to answer on time, and how many to roster.
Method: Erlang C. Offered load A (erlangs) = contacts x handle time / interval length. Probability of waiting is the Erlang C formula (computed through the stable Erlang B recursion). Service level = 1 - P(wait) x e^(-(N-A) x T / AHT). Average speed of answer = P(wait) x AHT / (N-A). Roster = agents / (1 - shrinkage), rounded up. Check: 200 calls an hour at 180 s (10 erlangs) with 14 agents gives 88.8% in 20 s, 17.4% waiting and 71.4% occupancy, which matches the worked example on Calcoid's Erlang C calculator. Erlang C assumes random (Poisson) arrivals, exponentially distributed handle times and callers who never hang up, so it tends to overstate waits a little for real queues. It ignores skills, breaks inside the interval and busy-hour spikes: plan with your real peak interval, not the daily average. Runs in your browser, nothing stored.
